Types of Temporary Buydowns

The type of temporary buydown you seek will depend on your individual needs. A borrower could reduce their rate for one year or up to three years if they so choose. The rate returns to the original fixed rate after the buydown period.

3-2-1 Buydown

Reduces the rate by 3% in year one, 2% in year two, and 1% in year three. After year three, the rate remains the same for the life of the loan.

2-1 Buydown

Reduces the rate by 2% in year one and 1% in year two. After the buydown period the rate returns to the original fixed rate.

1-1 Buydown

Reduces the rate by 1% for the first two years of the mortgage, then returns to the full rate for the remaining term.

1-0 Buydown

Reduces the rate by 1% for the first year only, then returns to the original fixed rate.

How Do You Calculate a Mortgage Buydown?

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a buydown work on a mortgage?

A temporary mortgage buydown is a lump sum paid by the builder or seller to temporarily reduce the interest rate of the mortgage for a specified time frame. Since the buydown lowers your interest rate, it will effectively reduce your overall monthly mortgage payment for the first few years of your mortgage.

What is the difference between a buydown and discount points?

Discount points, also known as mortgage points, are upfront fees a borrower can pay to get a reduced interest rate over the life of the loan. One discount point costs 1% of the loan amount and may lower the rate by up to 0.25%, depending on product and loan characteristics. Discount points are most effective when the borrower stays in the home long enough to break even on the cost.

Unlike discount points, a temporary buydown is typically paid by the seller or builder and reduces the interest rate only for the first few years of the mortgage. A temporary buydown can be a great option to help you ease into your mortgage payments or if you plan to move within a few years.

Is a buydown a good idea?

A temporary mortgage buydown can benefit both buyers and sellers. For buyers, it reduces the interest rate and therefore the monthly payment for the term of the buydown. Sellers like it because it offers a tangible financial benefit to the buyer without reducing the asking price of the home. The key is making sure the buydown is discussed during the offer and negotiation process so both parties are on the same page.
